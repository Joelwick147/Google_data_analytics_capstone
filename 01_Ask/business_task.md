# Phase 1: Ask — Business Task & Stakeholder Alignment

## 1. Background
Cyclistic is a bike-share program in Chicago featuring more than 5,800 bicycles and 600+ docking stations. The company offers three pricing plans:
-Single-Ride Passes(Casual Riders)
-Full-Day Passes (Casual Riders)
-Annual Memberships (Cyclistic Members)

Cyclistic's finance analysts have concluded that annual memberships represent predictable, stable, and highly profitable recurring revenue. Casual riders have high customer acquisition costs and their revenue is highly weather-dependent.

Rather than creating a marketing campaign that targets all-new customers, the Director of Marketing believes there is a strong opportunity to convert existing casual riders into annual members.

## 2. Business Task/requirements
Analyze 12 months of historical Cyclistic trip data to Identify the specific behavior profiles of casual riders to determine what triggers can convert them into annual subscribers, and provide data-backed recommendations to convert casual riders into annual members.
Executive Decision to be Made: Should the company launch a new subscription product
tailored specifically to recreational users, or run a generic city-wide marketing campaign?

## 3. Key Stakeholders
Lily Moreno (Director of Marketing): Responsible for the development of campaigns and initiatives to promote the bike-share program. She is the primary decision-maker for this project.

Cyclistic Marketing Analytics Team: A team of data analysts responsible for collecting, analyzing, and reporting data that helps guide Cyclistic marketing strategy.

Cyclistic Executive Team:The notoriously detail-oriented executive team that will decide whether to approve the recommended marketing program.


## 4. Questions to answer

1. What percentage of total trips are taken by members versus casual riders?
2. How does the average and median trip length (ride_length_mins) differ between the two groups?
3. How does ridership fluctuate by day of the week, hour of the day, and month/season?
4. Do casual riders prefer classic bikes, electric bikes, or docked bikes compared to members?
5. Which start and end stations generate the highest volume of casual trips versus member trips?

What is the statistical distribution of trip durations (mean/median) for
casual riders versus annual members?
What are the peak days and hours of traffic for each user segment?
What are the top 5 most heavily transited route paths for casual users?
